# Philosophy 300 — Chinese Native Prose Gate v0.1

Status: **FROZEN STYLE GATE**

Purpose: ensure the course is written as natural Chinese prose rather than translated English.

## Core rule

> Borrow the thinking structure of strong English-language philosophy writing, not its sentence structure.

The course may draw on:
- Anthony Gottlieb for source caution and historiographical restraint;
- Bertrand Russell for direct judgment and economy;
- Nigel Warburton for beginner accessibility.

Chinese prose must be written natively in Chinese.

A restrained Chinese cultural-essay register is acceptable as a tonal reference: natural unfolding, clear judgment, measured rhythm, some room for reflection, but no imitation of any living writer's distinctive phrasing or signature voice.

## Preferred Chinese qualities

- natural subject–verb structure;
- concrete verbs;
- short to medium sentences;
- clear logical sequence;
- judgment before abstraction;
- restrained rhythm;
- occasional reflective pause only when earned;
- cultural writing that still explains rather than performs sophistication.

## Translationese warnings

Flag and rewrite phrases like:
- “这个位置，是后人给他的”
- “形成了一种新的解释方式”
- “构成了哲学史上的重要起点”
- “对自然本身进行解释”
- “这使得他被后人视为……”
- “在某种意义上”
- “从这个角度来看”
- “值得注意的是”

These are not universally banned, but they must justify themselves.

Prefer ordinary Chinese verbs over nominalized structures.

Bad:
> 泰勒斯在哲学史中占据了一个重要位置，这使得他被后人视为第一位哲学家。

Better:
> 后来的哲学史，常常从泰勒斯讲起。

## Fact → Question → Explanation

When possible, let the question grow out of a concrete fact.

Preferred pattern:

> 我们今天关于泰勒斯最重要的一条记载，来自两百多年后的亚里士多德。
>
> 亚里士多德说，泰勒斯认为万物的根本是水。
>
> 为什么这样一句现在听来颇古怪的话，会让泰勒斯在哲学史里占据这么重要的位置？

This pattern is preferred over:
- asking the same question twice;
- explaining importance before showing the evidence;
- repeating “why he is placed first” in several formulations.

## Back-translation test

If a Chinese sentence feels as though it could be translated word-for-word back into English without losing its structure, inspect it for translationese.

Rewrite when necessary.

## Anti-performance rule

Do not copy the surface mannerisms of essayists.

Borrow:
- restraint;
- pacing;
- clarity;
- cultural texture.

Do not borrow:
- signature phrases;
- recognizable verbal tics;
- idiosyncratic metaphors;
- repeated rhetorical habits.

## Final test

Ask:

> Would an educated Chinese reader feel this was originally written in Chinese?

If no, FAIL.
