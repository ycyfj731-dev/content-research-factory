# Philosophy 300 — Anti-AI Prose Gate v0.1

Status: **HARD GATE**

Purpose: remove detectable model-written habits from public-facing Chinese prose without making the writing affected, literary for its own sake, or imitative of any specific writer.

## Core rule

> Do not make the prose sound “more human” by adding personality. Make it sound human by removing machine habits.

## Hard failures

A draft fails if it repeatedly shows any of the following:

### 1. Restating the same point
AI often says one idea three times:
- explanation;
- summary;
- “金句” version.

Keep the strongest version and delete the rest.

### 2. Empty transitions
Delete or rewrite phrases such as:
- 真正重要的是
- 更值得注意的是
- 这也正是
- 从某种意义上说
- 换句话说
- 也就是说
- 不难发现
- 仔细看
- 事情开始变得有趣
- 答案并没有那么复杂
- 这背后其实是

unless they carry real information.

### 3. Fake dramatic rhythm
Avoid:
- one-line fragments used only for emphasis;
- repeated “不是……而是……” structures;
- repeated “问题来了”;
- cliffhanger language where no real conceptual turn exists.

### 4. Symmetrical paragraphing
Do not make every paragraph:
- same length;
- same rhythm;
- same rhetorical shape;
- same ending cadence.

Human prose has irregular but controlled rhythm.

### 5. Generic profundity
Reject sentences that sound meaningful but add no claim.

Examples:
- “哲学从来不是关于答案，而是关于问题。”
- “真正留下来的，不只是思想，还有我们理解世界的方式。”

A sentence must earn its place by adding evidence, distinction, mechanism, or consequence.

### 6. Over-signposting
Do not constantly tell the reader what the paragraph is doing.

Avoid excessive:
- 首先 / 其次 / 最后
- 这里要注意
- 我们先看
- 接下来
- 这说明
- 由此可见

Use only when structure genuinely needs it.

### 7. Abstract noun inflation
Prefer verbs over chains like:
- 对……的理解
- ……的形成
- ……的构建
- ……的转向
- ……的展开
- ……的生成机制

Rewrite into who did what.

### 8. Over-clean logic
Do not force messy historical evidence into perfectly symmetrical logic.

If the evidence is incomplete, let the prose say it is incomplete.

### 9. Forced “reader friendliness”
Do not infantilize zero-baseline readers with:
- excessive analogies;
- fake conversational questions;
- “想象一下” every few paragraphs;
- over-explaining ordinary words.

Clear is not childish.

### 10. Tone imitation
Do not imitate the recognizable voice of any specific living writer.

Borrow only general strengths:
- clarity;
- historical caution;
- conceptual precision;
- rhythm;
- accessibility.

No signature diction, catchphrases, metaphors, or cadence.

## Preferred human signals

The prose should allow:
- short factual sentences beside longer explanatory ones;
- occasional plain transitions or no transition at all;
- concrete details before abstractions;
- slight asymmetry in paragraph length;
- direct admission of uncertainty;
- a sentence ending simply when the point is complete.

## Human-edit pass

After drafting, do one pass with these questions:

1. Which sentence sounds like it was written to “sound good” rather than say something?
2. Which paragraph repeats the one before it?
3. Which transition can be deleted with no loss?
4. Which abstract noun can become a verb?
5. Which rhetorical question can become a statement?
6. Which “golden sentence” is unearned?
7. Which sentence would a Chinese editor mark as translationese?
8. Where is the prose too perfectly symmetrical?

Delete first. Rewrite only when deletion creates a gap.

## Final test

Read the lesson without headings.

If the prose feels:
- over-smoothed;
- over-explained;
- over-signposted;
- rhythmically uniform;
- full of generic “insight” sentences;

FAIL.

The desired result is not “literary”.
It is **specific, controlled, native Chinese prose with visible human editorial judgment**.
