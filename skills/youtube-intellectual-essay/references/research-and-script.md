# Research, packaging and script

## 02 Competitor Research

Start with 3–5 directly relevant English long-form videos, using multiple channels and both recent and evergreen examples where available. Fewer inspectable examples are acceptable with an explicit coverage limitation, not invented substitutes. Record URL, title, channel, upload date, duration, observed views and observation date; leave inaccessible fields unknown. Record what was actually watched/read and the opening premise, narrative structure, visual technique and unanswered question.

Use public metrics as descriptive observations. Public view counts do not reveal impressions, CTR, retention, audience demographics or profitability. Do not manufacture a channel baseline; comparisons need a stated sample and age/context limitations. Treat transcripts as discovery aids, not citations proving claims. Extract useful craft patterns, then choose a specific contribution; do not rewrite a competitor's script or mirror its sequence of distinctive examples.

## 03 Source Research

Build a question-led evidence map before drafting claims. Prefer primary philosophical texts for attribution, original papers for specific results, and suitable reviews for the strength and limits of broader empirical claims. Use reputable scholarship to situate contested readings. Trace press coverage to the underlying work. Record bibliographic identity, URL/DOI, access date, exact locator and access level: full text, excerpt, abstract or metadata only.

For each central mechanism seek a credible competing explanation or boundary, not a ceremonial caveat. Distinguish desire, preference, conformity, social comparison, status signaling and imitation where relevant; do not collapse adjacent concepts. Do not force three famous thinkers into every essay. One well-explained framework plus a meaningful empirical comparison may suffice.

If a primary text cannot be inspected, mark attribution provisional and describe the access gap. Secondary commentary may support a clearly attributed secondary interpretation; it cannot certify an invented primary quotation. Keep leads separate from verified sources. Do not carry forward factual claims about platform policies or monetization from old conversations; verify current official guidance only if relevant to this episode.

## 04 Packaging

Draft three distinct English title/thumbnail pairs before the full script. For each, record the viewer question, the exact promise, the evidence that can deliver it, thumbnail focal point and why the pairing is different. Select a provisional pair and explain the tradeoff; do not call it an A/B winner without an actual experiment.

Titles should be comprehensible on their own. Thumbnails add a concrete visual tension rather than repeating the title. Try 0–4 words on the thumbnail, one focal relationship and strong hierarchy as starting defaults. Avoid universal claims, pseudo-neuroscience and deceptive images. Review any factual presupposition through Evidence Gate.

Write two short hook alternatives; choose the one that best connects a recognizable experience to the precise question. A hook should establish the promised inquiry within roughly 30 seconds. It must not need a fabricated number, quotation or personal anecdote. Hypothetical scenes begin with a signal such as “Imagine...” when needed.

## 05 Script

Use this adaptable spine: everyday puzzle → provisional mechanism → framework → evidence/example → objection/boundary → modern application → answer with a remaining question. Maintain one central inquiry; give each section a clear contribution and a transition that earns the next step. Use 2–3 frameworks only when each is necessary.

For 10–15 minutes, begin with approximately 1,300–2,100 spoken words at a tentative 130–150 words per minute, adjusted for pauses and complex visuals. Calculate `spoken words / chosen WPM + planned non-speech time`, then replace estimates with measured audio. This is a planning range, not permission to pad or rush. If the argument cannot sustain ten minutes, revise the scope or report the mismatch.

Write spoken English: concrete nouns, pronounceable clauses and varied sentence lengths. Introduce terminology after the mechanism is understandable. Place claim IDs in the annotated script, never in the clean TTS input. Keep explicit uncertainty and attribution in the spoken text, not just production notes. Avoid sermon-like self-help, deterministic “your brain is wired” claims and forced contrarianism.

Every section should resolve something and create a legitimate next question. Add attention resets where the reasoning changes: an objection, revealing comparison, concrete case or shift in scale. Do not impose arbitrary whooshes, zooms or withheld payoffs. The ending answers the opening question with appropriate limits; an optional next-video invitation follows that closure.

Deliver the annotated script and outline first. After Evidence Gate passes, freeze their versions and derive the clean VO text without adding claims. If later pronunciation edits change meaning, return that line to Evidence Gate.
