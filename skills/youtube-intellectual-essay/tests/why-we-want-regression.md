## Baseline planning fixture

Audience: curious English-speaking adults who recognize changing tastes, online aspiration and comparison, without prior philosophy study.

Working question: When another person's interest changes what seems desirable, what can a mimetic framework illuminate, and what does it leave unexplained?

Provisional editorial angle: distinguish wanting the object from wanting the position of the person who makes it appealing. Treat this as a hypothesis to investigate, not a universal account of all desires. Do not equate imitation with conformity, or philosophical interpretation with a measured causal effect.

### 05 Script: short opening sample

> Imagine scrolling past a jacket without a second thought. A week later, someone you admire wears it. Now you stop. You picture where you would wear it, what it might say about you, who you might be in it. The jacket is the same. So what changed? This imagined moment gives us a question: when we want something, how much of its appeal belongs to the object, and how much belongs to the people around it?

This sample illustrates C04 only; it does not certify a real psychological event or the rest of the proposed argument. The full source-dependent script stays DRAFT. Planned counterexamples include bodily needs, practical utility, prior experience and resistance to group preferences; investigate the distinctions rather than claiming a single example disproves a complex theory.
