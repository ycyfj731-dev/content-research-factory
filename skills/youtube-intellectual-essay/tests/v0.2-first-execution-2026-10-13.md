## Editorial fixture and reviewed artifact
Working question: How did clinical and academic terms enter ordinary language, and when does describing behavior become classifying a person?
Working thesis: "Psychological language gives us new ways to describe suffering; when these descriptions become fixed identities or judgments, they may also narrow how we see others." Interpretation, not a verified empirical conclusion.
Rival #1: Access to psychological vocabulary may help people recognize abuse or seek support, and broad availability can be beneficial.
Rival #2: Platform incentives and group signaling may explain usage patterns independently of diagnosis or psychological understanding.
Boundary: ADHD is a clinical diagnostic category, narcissist is used clinically and colloquially, patriarchy is not a clinical diagnosis, PUA has distinct origin and Chinese semantic history; **do not falsely group all terms as clinical diagnoses**.
Unverified: historical diffusion rates, trend causal mechanisms, prevalence and cross-cultural timelines. Evidence BLOCKED for all precise empirical claims until actual passages inspected.
Suggested stronger title promise: "When Did We Start Diagnosing Each Other?" However this does not cover all four examples equally; revise episode scope or use "When Did We Start Labeling Each Other?" before packaging PASS.
