## Why the four failed items now pass

### 01 — majority opinion / expertise
Source anchor: **Crito 47a–48a**.

The revised hook limits the claim to judgments about justice and preserves Socrates' analogy with listening to the knowledgeable person rather than the many. It no longer turns the passage into a universal slogan that "the majority cannot determine truth."

Boundary required in script:
- Socrates is not claiming that one dissenter is automatically right.
- The issue is whether numerical majority itself is a reason to treat an opinion as authoritative.

### 03 — cave
Source anchor: **Republic VII 514a ff.**

The prisoners are described as being in the cave **from childhood**, so "从小" is source-faithful. The lesson may use "reality" as an ordinary-language bridge, but the mechanism must return to Plato's hierarchy of appearance/opinion and intelligible knowledge rather than modern simulation theory.

Boundary required in script:
- Do not turn the cave into "Plato predicted The Matrix."
- Do not collapse it into a modern brain-processing claim.

### 05 — desire and lack
Source anchor: **Symposium 199c–201c**.

The revised hook reproduces Socrates' explicit puzzle: if desire concerns what one lacks, how can someone desire what they already possess? The answer in the dialogue is that present possessors can desire the **continued possession in the future** of what they now have.

Boundary required in script:
- Do not reduce the whole Symposium to "love is lack."
- This exchange prepares the move into Diotima's account of Eros; it is not the complete theory of love.

### 10 — philosophy and death
Source anchor: **Phaedo 64a–67e**.

The revised hook preserves the strangeness of the source claim instead of psychologizing it as "learning to face death." In the dialogue, death is defined as separation of soul and body, and philosophical practice is linked to freeing the soul from bodily distraction in pursuit of truth.

Boundary required in script:
- Do not reinterpret this as modern grief counseling or death-anxiety management.
- Do not imply endorsement of suicide; the dialogue explicitly distinguishes readiness for death from taking one's own life.

## Interpretation

The gate passed the retest without relaxing any criterion.

The main lesson is methodological:

1. A good hook can remain vivid without widening the source claim.
2. Source fidelity often improves the hook by preserving the original philosophical strangeness.
3. The highest-risk distortions are not factual errors; they are attractive conceptual substitutions.
4. Style should be applied only after the gate, never before.
