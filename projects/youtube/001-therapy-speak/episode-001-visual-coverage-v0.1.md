# Episode 001 — Visual Coverage Map v0.3 (script-locked audit)

**Basis:** [production-locked V6.1](episode-001-v6.1-production-locked.md) (six chapters, approved Ginapp/Brady/ending revisions).  
**Stage:** EDITORIAL DRAFT; source-rights checks, VO timing, actual design and PPT rendering NOT RUN.  
**Key principle:** this is a shot/visual *coverage* plan, not a 34-slide slideshow. A shot can contain multiple compositional key states, and multiple narration sentences can share one evolving visual. Never synchronize one sentence to one hard cut.

## On-screen selection rules

- **Evidence:** names/dates/scope/qualification must appear succinctly when facts are central; supporting methodological nuance stays primarily in narration.
- **Concept motion:** show relationships and shifts that listeners would have difficulty tracking in audio alone.
- **Illustrative scene:** place ordinary human conditions on screen, but never pass mock social accounts or testimony off as documentary sources.
- **Voice/Hold:** no extra graphic whenever an already established scene can remain and subtly progress.
- **Captions:** independently timed from narration, with lower screen safe area reserved; text within scene should not echo the whole VO.
- **Visual style:** white background, restrained black typography, consistent asset-based border collie *only where narratively useful*; no crude SVG redraw.
- **Timing:** absent actual final English VO; all durations withheld rather than inventing timestamps. First measure aligned final audio.

## Logic Review 02 — editorial audit (2026-10-13)

**Verdict:** v0.1 was a useful coverage pass, but the following constraints are required before static slide design. This is a review of the visual interpretation against the supplied V6.1 script, **not** an independent external fact-check of the original studies.

### Argument and evidence boundaries

- **Chapter 1:** avoid inventing a continuous real-life case that the narrator never describes. The opening is a witty survey of familiar labels; the first clinical distinction is casual `narcissist` vs diagnosis of NPD, not a list of diagnostic criteria.
- **Chapter 2:** preserve the actual argument order: 2022 Merriam-Webster attention → 1938/1944 origin → pre-social-media 1990s circulation → contemporary video/forum/dinner relay → 2014 general linguistic diffusion → 2026 context-sensitive meanings → gaslighting boundary. Their ordering is a narrative progression, **not** a demonstration that Eisenstein causally establishes the Iacob & Uban mechanism. The +1,740% is dictionary lookups against a prior year; never represent it as real-world usage frequency or fabricate a historical chart.
- **Chapter 3:** preserve the same-study **benefits and risks** in Ginapp; no invented first-person participant quotation, and 43 self-reported ADHD diagnoses must not imply 43 independent verifications. Brown's nine studies concern professional jargon/status; the coworker comparison is an editorial illustration, not direct experimental evidence for therapy-speak.
- **Chapter 4:** distinguish (a) clinical questioning of specific behavior, (b) a fast online label, (c) the social visibility of agreement, (d) the *different-domain* empirical finding about political tweet diffusion, and (e) the philosophical argument about perceived professional authority. These are **not a proved single causal chain**. The comment demonstration needs source facts, interpretive category and public agreement as separate visual layers. Hypothetical sentiment marks must not look like scraped metrics.
- **Chapter 5:** personal support can help while working conditions remain; do not show policies being repaired unless the narration actually asserts a reform outcome. WHO's occupational classification is source material, not proof of a particular illustrative worker's clinical state.
- **Chapter 6:** compare social *functions* of vocabulary across settings, not a claim that one specific word means exactly the same thing in a support group, public dispute and workplace. The hypothetical clip is not a real diagnosis.

### Priority before PPT

1. **P0 — factual distinction:** Create an evidence-label matrix for the eleven research/official reference clusters, with field `source scope`, `what the VO claims`, `what the on-screen design can claim`, and `rights/clearance`. Real screenshots remain optional until checked.
2. **P0 — script-to-frame coverage:** Tie each unit to verbatim start and end sentences from the locked script. The current abbreviated anchors are insufficient to prove that every voiceover sentence is accounted for or that no screen depicts a claim prematurely.
3. **P1 — editable key states:** Design genuine 16:9 static before/after compositional frames for every meaningful transition, not one static slide per sentence or decorative labels flying around.
4. **P1 — motion transitions:** Specify exact object continuity and state changes within chapters 2, 4 and 5. Cross-chapter hard cuts are allowed when conceptually appropriate; forced morphs are not.
5. **P1 — timing:** Generate the actual VO and time-align each paragraph; only then budget individual states, pauses, and transition beats.

**Gate:** Full visual coverage remains **DRAFT**, and PPT production should not be called production-ready until P0 tasks pass.

## Editorial motion system

1. **WORD TOKEN** moves between media containers: relationship story → screen label → question → comment verdict → workplace/society context. Use the same graphic identity; transform position and meaning, not just opacity.
2. **QUESTION MARK** signals limits to certainty. A clinical-sounding label is not a documented assessment.
3. **CONTEXT FIELD** represents the evidence left out by compression. In Chapter 4 it remains stable even as simulated approvals accumulate.
4. **SOURCE CARDS** are short traceable bibliographic glimpses (study population, dates, population type, key finding). Do not turn the dense Chapter 2 into four static paper screenshots.
5. **SCENE RECURRENCE** reuse established actions, avoiding filler or needless stock montage.
6. **PACING** opening brisk but readable; history uses a flowing media network; Chapter 3 holds for ambiguity/support; Chapter 4 grants enough time to watch reasoning; Chapter 5 returns to everyday concrete conditions; ending recombines prior visual motifs without new evidence.
