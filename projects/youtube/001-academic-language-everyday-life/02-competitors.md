## Adjacent long-form reference

**American Psychological Association, Speaking of Psychology, Episode 325 — “The rise of ‘therapy speak’” (2025, 34 min).**  
Public transcript: https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/therapy-speak

Observed craft pattern:
- hook uses familiar labels (“narcissist,” “gaslighting,” “boundaries”);
- immediately frames both benefit and misuse;
- establishes that popular psychological vocabulary predates TikTok (example: codependency in the 1990s);
- expert interview format keeps the inquiry balanced rather than purely condemnatory.

## Repeated packaging patterns

1. **“Everyone is X now”** — high-recognition label + cultural complaint.
2. **Weaponization / misuse** — terms framed as social weapons.
3. **TikTok as cause shorthand** — often asserted more strongly than evidence warrants.
4. **Narcissist / gaslighting / boundaries** dominate; sociology and political theory are mostly absent.
5. Most pieces frame the issue as “correct vs incorrect use,” rather than as a broader circulation/translation process.

## Distinct editorial angle

Our episode will not be “TikTok ruined psychology.”

Instead:

> **Different kinds of concepts migrate into everyday language through different routes. Once ordinary users adopt them, the concepts can become tools for naming experience—but they can also expand, mutate and lose context.**

The four-case design is the differentiation:
- ADHD = diagnostic language;
- gaslighting = drama → colloquial/psychological language;
- patriarchy = social/feminist theory → digital meme culture;
- PUA = subculture → cross-language semantic expansion.
