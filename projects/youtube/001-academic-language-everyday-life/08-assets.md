## Beat-by-beat acquisition notes

### B01–B03
Do not use generic stock footage of people scrolling phones. The opening should feel like a language/culture essay, not a mental-health PSA. Typography and real interface fragments are enough.

### B04–B06 — ADHD
Use official/public-health and peer-reviewed study material. Recreate charts rather than copying publication figures when the data can be faithfully transcribed. Always show sample size and “cross-sectional” qualifier where relevant.

### B08–B10 — NPD / narcissist
Avoid faces entirely. No villain imagery, mirrors, masks or “toxic man” clichés. The visual story is **category structure**, not character judgment.

### B12–B14 — Patriarchy
Use scholarship, title pages, bibliographic records and original structural diagrams. Avoid synthetic historical feminist protest footage. If real protest photography is used later, license/attribution must be verified asset-by-asset.

### B15–B16 — PUA
Prefer Chinese headlines, abstracts and terminology maps. If English subtitles/translations are added, mark them as translations. Do not use invented chat screenshots.

### B17–B18 — Mechanisms
These should be the visual intellectual payoff of the episode. Spend design effort here: the diagrams must be memorable enough that viewers can mentally reuse them.

### B19–B22 — Benefit, cost, conclusion
Avoid “happy person healed / sad person confused” stock tropes. Keep the ending conceptual and editorial.

## Rights categories

### Green — preferred
- original diagrams/typography
- public-domain archive
- official government/public-health pages for brief quoted/excerpted commentary
- clearly licensed Creative Commons assets with terms documented

### Yellow — needs contextual review
- news screenshots
- journal/publisher page screenshots
- search-engine/social-platform interface crops
- book covers / article thumbnails

Use only where necessary for commentary, keep excerpts limited, attribute clearly, and preserve source URLs internally.

### Red — avoid unless licensed
- commercial stock without license
- copyrighted documentary footage copied from YouTube
- movie/TV clips used as decoration
- influencer/TikTok clips downloaded and reused without permission
- AI recreations that could be mistaken for real historical footage
