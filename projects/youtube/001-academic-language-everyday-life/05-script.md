## Central inquiry

**How do concepts from expert, theoretical and niche worlds become everyday language, and what happens to their meaning when ordinary people use them to interpret life?**

## Working thesis

> The internet did not simply “popularize academic jargon.” It accelerated a broader process in which concepts from very different origins become portable tools for naming and interpreting everyday experience. That migration can increase recognition and literacy, but it also invites semantic expansion, context loss and overconfident labeling.

This is a synthesis/argument, not a directly measured empirical finding.

## Beat outline

### B01 — Ordinary language sounds more conceptual
Opening montage: ADHD / gaslighting / trauma / attachment style / patriarchy / PUA.

Purpose: establish recognition without claiming simultaneous origin or prevalence.

Claims: C01, C02.

### B02 — The obvious story is wrong
Reject: “TikTok took academic terms and made them viral.”

Show origin-path map:
- ADHD → diagnostic medicine/psychology;
- gaslighting → stage play/film → colloquial/psychological use;
- patriarchy → feminist theory/politics → digital meme culture;
- PUA → pickup-artist subculture → Chinese emotional-control usage.

Claims: C03–C07.

### B03 — Case 1: ADHD and self-description
Use current research on ADHD TikTok content and comment self-attribution.

Key move: distinguish **visibility/self-recognition** from formal diagnosis. Avoid saying social media causes ADHD or diagnosis rates.

Claims: C04, C05.

### B04 — Case 2: Gaslighting and semantic widening
Trace the 1938 play origin and APA's note that the term is now used more generally.

Key move: this case proves that popular psychology vocabulary can be assembled from culture as well as laboratories/clinics.

Claim: C03.

### B05 — Case 3: Patriarchy and meme portability
Use Hill & Allen to show documented digital resurgence and the tradeoff of meme brevity: community-forming usefulness alongside reduced room for theoretical complexity/intersectional nuance.

Claim: C06.

### B06 — Case 4: PUA as cross-language mutation
Show Western pickup-artist origin, Chinese pickup-industry uptake, then broadening toward emotional manipulation/control.

Key move: translation and local social events can alter a term as much as “popularization.”

Claim: C07.

### B07 — So what is the common mechanism?
Introduce three mechanisms with different evidence strength:

1. **Naming** — a term can make an experience easier to recognize and discuss. (interpretive; S02/S09)
2. **Lay appropriation / double hermeneutic** — social-science concepts can be taken up by the people they describe, changing self-understanding and sometimes the concept itself. (S09)
3. **Semantic expansion / concept creep** — some harm/mental-health concepts broaden over time. (S08/S10)

Do **not** add an unsupported fourth mechanism such as “the algorithm rewards diagnostic labels” unless later evidence is obtained.

Claims: C08, C09.

### B08 — The benefit is real
A concept can reduce isolation, provide language, aid recognition and make previously private experiences discussable.

Frame as supported interpretation/expert view, not a universal outcome.

### B09 — The cost is also real
Broader/looser meanings can:
- flatten distinctions;
- encourage lay diagnosis;
- turn description into verdict;
- lose original scope and context.

Use ADHD self-attribution data and concept-creep literature carefully.

### B10 — Counterexample to panic
Popularized expert vocabulary is not a TikTok invention. APA experts point to “codependency” in the 1990s; psychoanalytic/self-help language circulated long before short video.

Purpose: prevent generational moral panic.

Claim: C02.

### B11 — Earned ending
Answer:

> We are not simply becoming “more academic.” We are building everyday life out of portable concepts. The important question is not whether ordinary people are allowed to use them, but whether we still remember what each concept can—and cannot—explain.

Remaining question for future episode:
**What happens when a useful concept becomes so broad that almost anything can fit inside it?**
