## Everyday tension

Ordinary conversation increasingly uses labels such as **ADHD**, **gaslighting**, **patriarchy**, **trauma**, **attachment style**, and—in Chinese internet culture—**PUA**. These labels can make previously vague experiences legible, but they can also compress complex clinical, theoretical or cultural ideas into portable judgments.

## Central question

**How do concepts that originate in medicine, psychology, sociology, feminist theory, media or subculture become everyday language—and what changes when ordinary people begin using them to interpret themselves and other people?**

This replaces the weaker premise that all such words are “academic terms” or that they became popular at the same moment.

## Provisional answer

The best-supported working explanation is not a single causal story. Several processes interact:

1. professional/theoretical concepts can give a memorable name to lived experience;
2. media and platforms make those terms portable and repeatable;
3. lay users reinterpret concepts for new situations;
4. meanings can broaden, flatten or shift as circulation increases;
5. the migration can improve literacy and recognition while also producing overextension and mislabeling.

## Counterargument / boundary

- Popularized expert language is not new; earlier waves included psychoanalytic and self-help vocabulary.
- Not every popular label began in academia: **gaslighting** came from drama before psychology; **PUA** came from pickup-artist subculture, not a university discipline.
- Evidence that social media distributes a concept does **not** by itself prove that recommendation algorithms caused the change.
- Broader meanings are not automatically wrong; semantic expansion can reflect legitimate recognition of previously unnamed experiences.

## Concrete cases

1. **ADHD** — diagnostic category becoming part of online self-description.
2. **Gaslighting** — drama-derived term later adopted into psychological/relationship discourse and widened in everyday use.
3. **Patriarchy** — feminist theoretical concept with documented online resurgence through meme culture.
4. **PUA** — English pickup-artist subculture term that underwent major semantic expansion in Chinese internet discourse toward emotional manipulation and control.

## Fresh angle

Most existing “therapy-speak” coverage stays inside mental-health vocabulary and asks whether people misuse clinical terms. This episode broadens the frame: **what happens when concepts from different knowledge systems become lay interpretive tools?** PUA is deliberately retained as a counterexample to prevent the false thesis that every term moved directly from academia to TikTok.

## Why this needs 12–16 minutes

The argument requires:
- a historical distinction between different origin paths;
- four contrasting case studies;
- a mechanism section (naming, portability, reinterpretation, semantic expansion);
- a benefits/risks section;
- an objection showing this process predates TikTok.
